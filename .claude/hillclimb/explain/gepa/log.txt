  diagnosis: Three general patterns. (1) The checking phrase is used as filler: answers open with "Let me check..." even when no tool was called, often on simple items such as sliders, status tags or dropdowns whose options are already visible. The current rule is too easy to ignore and does not say which items never need inspecting. (2) Two opposite problems with settings. Answers invent mechanics the page does not show, such as slider direction, units stated as page facts, or saving behaviour. They also leave out the quantity a well-known term clearly implies, such as a duration and its usual unit, or a key part of the term's everyday meaning. The prompt needs to separate the meaning of a term, which can be explained fully from common knowledge, from the control's mechanics, which should not be invented. When a unit is only inferred, it should be stated as typical rather than as a fact about this page. (3) Preset menus are described only as "applies settings".
